Privacy Policy — Reveal Blast

Last updated: 29 July 2026

Reveal Blast ("the app", "we") is a block puzzle game. We built it to respect your attention and your privacy. This policy explains, in plain language, what happens with your data.

The short version

We do not ask for your name, email, or an account. Your game progress is saved only on your device. We collect anonymous statistics about how the game is played so we can improve it, plus crash reports when something goes wrong — and you can switch both off in Settings. Some versions show a small number of ads, and there is a one-time "Remove Ads" purchase. That's it.

What we store on your device

Your game progress, settings, and purchases are stored locally on your device. We do not run our own servers, and your saved game is never uploaded anywhere. Deleting the app removes it.

Anonymous game statistics

To understand whether the game actually works — whether puzzles are too hard, whether a screen is confusing, whether a feature is used at all — we send anonymous event data to PostHog, an analytics service acting as our processor.

What is sent is a fixed, short list of gameplay events, such as: a game started or ended (with the score, lines cleared, and how long it lasted), a board fully cleared, a daily puzzle completed, an item bought with in-game coins, and whether a purchase or restore succeeded. Each event carries a random identifier generated on your device the first time you open the app, so we can tell one player's session from another's without knowing who anyone is.

Just as important is what is not sent:

  • No location. We explicitly instruct PostHog not to look up your location from your IP address, and not to record the address itself.
  • No free text. Events carry only fixed labels and numbers. There is no way for a message, a name, or anything you type to end up in them.
  • No advertising identifier, and no profile built about you.
  • No screen recording, no session replay, and no tracking of individual taps.
  • Nothing linked to an identity, because the app has no accounts and never asks who you are.

The random identifier is stored in the app's own storage and disappears when you delete the app. See PostHog's privacy policy at posthog.com/privacy.

You can turn this off. Settings › Privacy › "Share anonymous statistics" stops it, and stops crash reports with it. Switching it off is a real off switch, not a filter: from the next launch the analytics software is not started at all, so nothing is sent and no identifier is used. The same screen shows you your anonymous identifier, so you can quote it if you ever want to ask us about your data.

Crash reports

When the app crashes, a report is sent through the same service so we can find and fix the bug. It contains technical information about the failure — where in the code it happened, the device model, and the app and iOS version. It does not contain your saved game or anything you typed.

Ads

Some versions of the game show a small number of ads, through AppLovin MAX and its advertising partners. To serve and measure ads, these partners may process device information such as an advertising identifier, approximate location, and app-usage data. Where required, we ask your permission first (Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt) before any tracking — you can decline, and the game plays exactly the same. Buying "Remove Ads" turns ads off entirely. See AppLovin's privacy policy at applovin.com/privacy.

This is separate from the anonymous statistics above: ad partners are their own companies with their own policies, and the analytics described above never receive your advertising identifier.

Purchases

The "Remove Ads" purchase is handled entirely by Apple's App Store. We never see or store your payment details. We do record, anonymously, that a purchase or restore happened and whether it succeeded.

Children

Reveal Blast is intended for a general audience and is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Your choices

You can switch off anonymous statistics and crash reports in the game's own Settings › Privacy. You can decline ad tracking when asked, reset or limit your advertising identifier in iOS Settings › Privacy & Security, or buy "Remove Ads" to stop ads. Deleting the app removes everything stored on your device, including the random analytics identifier — after that, nothing further is sent, and a reinstall starts from a new identifier that cannot be connected to the old one. If you have a question or a request about your data, email us at the address below.
